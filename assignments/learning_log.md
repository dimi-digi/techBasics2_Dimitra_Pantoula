# Week 3
This week's lecture kicked off by introducing the duty cycle, which basically means the sum of on- and off-time of the circuit. I found this fact especially interesting and my attention was caught by the device, that displays it, namely the oscilloscope. The duty cycles can have variations of on-time, e.g. 25%, 50% or even 75%. Another part of the content that stuck with me was the distinction between analog and digital pins. Basically, digital pins deal in two states only: HIGH and LOW and are mainly used for turning an LED on and off. Analog pins, on the other hand, can read continuous range of voltages. The board's ADC converter (analog to digital converter) turns the voltage into numbers. Analog pins are mostly used for sensors. The experimentation with the photoresistor and seeing how its light sensitivity influenced the brightness of the LED was also quite fun. However, I had some issues with coordinating the coding to the make the circuit function, especially with the INPUT_PULLUP, so i need to revise more on that.
